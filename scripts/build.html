Does the drug reshape how Aβ42 moves?

Adaptive molecular-dynamics simulations of the Alzheimer’s peptide Aβ42, studied three ways: on its own, with the phase-3 drug tramiprosate (TMP), and with its metabolite SPA. Ensemble Lens puts all three systems side by side in every view, so the effect of a drug candidate on the conformational ensemble reads at a glance instead of from a stack of separate static charts.

1 · Conformational balance

task T1 · overview

The share of all simulated time each system spends in each Markov state: its stationary distribution. A drug that stabilises or destabilises a conformation shows up as a shift in these bars. Whiskers are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over trajectories. State numbers are matched across the three systems by CoVAMPnet, so state k refers to the same conformational basin in each. The glyph under each tick is the free peptide's conformer for that state, reused under all three systems. The matched basins are close but not identical, and some match better than others, so hover a glyph for the measured cross-system agreement.

2 · Sampling coverage over time

task T1 · convergence check

A convergence and coverage diagnostic, not real-time kinetics. The horizontal axis is position within the short simulations (aligned by start time), the band is the fraction of trajectories in each state there, and the sparkline above is how many trajectories are still active. Where the band settles and coverage stays thick, the population estimate is trustworthy; where coverage thins toward the right, treat it with caution.

3 · Transition structure

task T2 · comparison

Probability of moving from the row state to the column state over a 40-frame lag. These states are highly metastable: the dashed diagonal (staying put) is ~97 to 100%, so the peptide rarely switches inside one short simulation, which is exactly why adaptive sampling has to re-seed (see Panel 4). The tiny off-diagonal switching probabilities are therefore colour-scaled to their own range so the pattern is visible: darkest = the most common escape route. Switch to difference vs free to see how each drug re-routes them. Blue = more likely with the drug, red = less.

Model validation, implied timescales. A Markov model is only trustworthy at a lag where the relaxation timescales stop depending on the lag. Each line is one relaxation timescale of the model, numbered from the slowest; the dashed marker is the lag used above (τ = 40 frames). Curves that flatten to the right mean the model is self-consistent there.

4 · Adaptive-sampling genealogy

novel · the “adaptive” view

Adaptive sampling spawns each new simulation from a promising frame of an earlier one. Each dot is a simulation, placed by the epoch (round) it was launched in and coloured by the state it spends most time in; lines connect a simulation to its seed. This exposes where the sampling chose to explore, a structure the standard charts discard entirely.

5 · Ensemble strip matrix

task T1 · every trajectory

One row per simulation, coloured frame-by-frame by its assigned state. This is the whole ensemble at once, the raw material behind every chart above, revealing when states appear, how persistent they are, and how coverage builds up over time.

What are the states, and where does the drug bind?

The panels above treat each Markov state as a label. These connect the label back to the 3D peptide, reconstructed from the full atomistic trajectories, so a state becomes a shape, and a drug effect becomes a set of amino acids.

7 · Where the drug binds

novel · T3 · residue interactions

How often each amino acid along the Aβ42 sequence contacts a drug molecule, per Markov state. Because the box holds 100 ligand copies, some contact happens by chance; specific binding subtracts that background (a contact map built from time-decorrelated frames), leaving only engagement above chance. The coloured bands mark the aggregation-critical regions. This is where the two drugs part company. Switch between observed and specific: raw contact makes them look alike, both piled onto the N-terminus, but once chance is removed tramiprosate engages acidic side chains almost exclusively (E3, D7, E22, D1, D23, E11) while SPA engages only basic ones (R5, K28, K16). The reason is the ligands themselves: tramiprosate is a zwitterion carrying a cationic amine, SPA a dianion with none, so it can only salt-bridge to Arg and Lys. K16 opens the KLVFF core and K28 forms the D23-K28 salt bridge of the aggregation-competent turn.

8 · The binding pocket

novel · T3 · the drug in place

The trajectories carry all-atom coordinates for the ligand as well as the peptide, so the binding site can be shown directly rather than inferred. For each state we take the frame in which the nearest of the 100 ligand copies makes the most residue contacts, and draw that pose: the Cα backbone in grey, the drug molecule in ball-and-stick, and the peptide atoms lining the site. The backbone is thickened and tinted by how often each residue touches the drug across the whole state, so a persistent site is visible as a thick coloured stretch rather than one lucky frame. Because Aβ42 is disordered, the site is a fuzzy, sequence-defined pocket, not a rigid cleft, which is exactly why it has to be read as an ensemble.

9 · Representative shapes

task T3 · abstract → spatial

Each state is a disordered ensemble, not one fold, so several superposed Cα backbones are overlaid (N-terminus blue to C-terminus amber); the more they diverge, the more disordered the state. Drag any structure to rotate them together. Red beads mark the top drug-contact residues, and the Rg and mean per-residue flexibility (RMSF) are noted.

10 · Conformational landscape

novel · T3 · where the drug pushes

Every frame placed by compactness (radius of gyration, x) and aggregation-prone β-content (fraction of residues in β-strand, y), as a free-energy map (darker = more populated = lower energy). Rings mark each Markov state’s centre. Comparing the three maps shows, in one view, how each drug moves the whole ensemble across conformational space. Read as a folding funnel: dark wells are basins, rings are the Markov states that sit in them. The curve along the top is the distribution of compactness on its own, with the free peptide dashed in behind each drug for direct comparison, because the shift in compactness is hard to judge from the 2D density alone.

11 · β-strand propensity

task T3 · aggregation readout

The fraction of frames each residue sits in a β-strand, averaged over the ensemble (population-weighted across states), for all three systems. β-structure in the CHC (17-21) and C-terminus (30-42) is what drives Aβ42 aggregation, so comparing the three areas is the most direct readout of whether a drug lowers aggregation-prone structure. Bands mark those regions. We state the assumption plainly: we take more β-strand in these regions as predictive of more aggregation. These simulations contain a single peptide, so they cannot show aggregation directly; the β-content is a structural proxy, not an aggregation assay.
